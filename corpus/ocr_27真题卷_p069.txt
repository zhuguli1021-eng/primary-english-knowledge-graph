Six hundred years ago, a restaurant was not a place. It was a thin soup. People in France would cook
ham or chicken in a pot with water, vegetables, and some other ingredients. If your stomach hurt, these soups
would help you feel better.
In 1766, Mathurin Roze de Chantoiseau opened a House of Health in Paris. He invented the restaurant.
At first, he just wanted to create a place where people could gather, eat well, share ideas, and do business.
As these "meeting and eating" places became popular, they turned into restaurants-places with great menus,
private #4A#J) tables, and polite waiters. But at that time restaurants could only be found in Paris. It took
about 75 years for restaurants to appear in other European countries and the United States.
Before restaurants opened in the United States, people ate at bars or inns. They are places where people
could sleep, eat, drink and dance. Food was served family style on big plates. In the 1830s, the brothers
John and Pietro Delmonico from Switzerland brought the first real restaurant to the US. They opened
Delmonico's. In the end, many more Delmonico's restaurants opened in New York City.
France did do a lot for the food around the world. It not only brought us a simple soup and a place to eat,
but also taught us how to cook. Even the white apron (| ) and tall chef's hat were originally (fix J)
designed in France.
