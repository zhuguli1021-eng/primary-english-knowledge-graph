protect 50,000 hectares of eucalyptus forests (koalas’ main habitat) and plant 10,000 more eucalyptus trees.
Leo believes that young people have unique voices—we can use social media to spread awareness faster than
ever before. These young conservationists are inspiring millions of teenagers to take action. They show that
with determination and teamwork, we can all contribute to a greener future.

（三）Read and complete.（共10分）
Tu Youyou, a world-renowned pharmacologist and Nobel Prize laureate, was born in Ningbo, Zhejiang
Province in 1930. From an early age, she showed great interest in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM). After
graduating from Peking University Medical School in 1955, she devoted herself to researching herbal medicines
to treat infectious diseases. In the 1960s, malaria was a deadly disease that killed millions of people
worldwide, especially in Africa and Southeast Asia. At that time, there was no effective drug to cure it. Tu
Youyou led a research team and spent over 190 days collecting and studying more than 2,000 TCM
prescriptions. She faced countless challenges—many experiments failed, and she even suffered from health
problems due to long-term exposure to toxic chemicals. However, she never gave up. In 1971, after 190
failures, she finally discovered artemisinin, a powerful drug that can cure malaria. Over the years, artemisinin
has saved more than 200 million lives around the world. In 2015, Tu Youyou became the first Chinese female
scientist to win the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for her discovery. She continued her research even in
her 80s, exploring new uses of artemisinin in treating other diseases. Tu Youyou once said, “Perseverance and
curiosity are the key to scientific success. We should learn from traditional wisdom but also keep innovating.”
She is a role model for all young people who dream of making contributions to science.
