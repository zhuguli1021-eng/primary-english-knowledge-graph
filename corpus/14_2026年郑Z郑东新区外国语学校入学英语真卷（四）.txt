A
In the UK, we have English lessons in secondary school every day. We
focus on a different book each term, studying its grammar, vocabulary（词汇）and writing style. For
this spring term, we are working on British playwright William Shakespeare’s play A Midsummer
Night’s Dream(《仲夏夜之梦》).
A week before our first lesson, we went to the library to borrow copies of the book. We don’t have to buy
our own books, as they are reused and kept in the library. I think it’s a good way to protect the environment.
I find studying these books is really fun! At the beginning of the lesson, our teacher prepares some word
games for us to play, such as crosswords and filling in the blanks. The games last for five to ten minutes.
Afterwards, we will either act out some of the scenes(场景) in the play or read them aloud. This is an
effective(有效的) way to learn the words of Shakespeare. His plays were written for the stage(舞台),
after all!
Most of our time is spent doing worksheets. We spend about 20 to 30 minutes working on them. The
worksheets are based on scenes in the play. We either analyze(分析) a phrase or sentence, or make our own
quizzes(小测试) and poems. I love making up my own poems.
Sometimes, my teacher will ask if anyone is willing to do a presentation(展示) for our next English
lesson. I have already done two of them. You need to prepare a lot of things, such as an introduction, a task
or game to get everyone warmed up, as well as prepare the main content(内容) of the presentation.

Time flies when you play on your smartphone. But when you sit down to work and study, an hour feels
like a year. ______
It is all because of something small called dopamine(多巴胺) which is a “pleasure chemical” that makes
people feel good. Our brains release it when we eat yummy food, after we exercise or when we are praised.
Compared with studying, eating chocolate can make your brain release 150% more dopamine, and
exercising for 30 minutes raises it by 200%. But playing on smartphones creates much more dopamine. For
example, watching short videos gives a 250% increase, and checking new messages makes dopamine jump by
400%—much higher than studying. But why?
When we go through social media, we don’t know what we’ll see next, or who will follow our posts. This
uncertainty makes the dopamine active. Studying doesn’t provide such uncertainty. Without the excitement of
“gambling(赌博)”, we get bored much more easily.
Can we do something to change that? According to a study, apps can become less attractive if we turn off
notifications or change the screen colours to black and white. We can also set time limits on apps to avoid
overusing them.
As for studying, you could check your progress daily or weekly and reward(奖赏) yourself for it. You
could also explore interesting topics related to your textbook. Use what you’ve learned to make a difference in
the real world. This will make knowledge much more interesting to learn.
