Ⅰ. 阅读理解。（共30分）
A
Jane is seven years old and Billy is eleven. They often fight(打架) at home.
“Mum, Billy doesn’t give my book back!”
“It’s not yours. It’s mine.”
I don’t know how to stop them at first, but then I have an idea.
I give them some “punishments” when they fight. But those “punishments” are not really
punishments—just nice things for each other. For example, when Jane begins a fight, she must clean Billy’s
room. When Billy begins a fight, he must give Jane a hug(拥抱). They must do many things like this for
each other.
I write these “punishments” on a round board. When one of them begins a fight, they must flip(翻转)
the board and get their “punishments”.
Jane and Billy think this game is great fun and it works well. Now they get on well. When children do
something wrong, we can do this kind of interesting game—to let them know how to be nice to each other.

B
Sally was a student. It was going to be her mother’s birthday. She wanted to buy her a gift. It would be
nice and useful but not expensive.
She went shopping after a quick and simple lunch. After she looked for half an hour, she decided to buy
a black umbrella.
She thought, “Mum can carry it when she is wearing clothes of any colour.” So she bought a lovely
black umbrella and took it to school with her until her classes finished.
On her way home, on the train she felt hungry because she had so little for lunch. So she went to the
buffet car(餐车). She left the umbrella on her seat.
But when she got back, it was lost!
Sally started to cry. The other passengers(乘客) felt very sorry for her and asked what was the matter.
She told them she had bought a black umbrella for her mother, but it was lost, and she had to get off at the
next station. After the three passengers heard this, they asked her for her mother’s address(地址), and they
said they would send the umbrella to her if they found it.
And now a week passed. Sally got a letter from her mother. It said, “Thank you very much for your lovely
gifts, but why do you send me three black umbrellas?”
